You are designing the official homepage for **VisitSabah.net — The Official Sabah Visitor Experience Gateway**, an initiative under the Sabah Ministry of Tourism, Culture and Environment (KePKAS) ecosystem.

Act simultaneously as:

- a world-class destination-brand creative director;
- a senior web architect;
- a visitor-first UX strategist;
- a mobile-first UI designer;

This is a **static homepage design assignment**, not a functioning prototype. Show important interaction states as separate annotated design states or component examples, but do not create unrelated pages.

# 2. PLATFORM POSITIONING

VisitSabah.net is:

- an official visitor-experience gateway;
- a destination inspiration and planning platform;
- a trusted route to verified practical information;
- a discovery layer connecting places, activities, itineraries, events and stories;
- the permanent digital home for the Five Wonders of Sabah;
- the integrated digital home for SET, STEP and Visit Sabah 2027;
- a bridge to official agencies and recognised tourism services; and
- a multilingual, accessible and mobile-first public-facing platform.

VisitSabah.net is **not**:

- a corporate ministry homepage;
- an online travel agency;
- a generic tourism blog;
- an unstructured photo gallery;
- a temporary Visit Sabah 2027 microsite;
- a page dominated by government organisation charts;
- a collection of unrelated campaign banners; or
- an interface that looks like a generic AI-generated travel template.

The governing brand impression should be:

> **Cinematic Sabah inspiration, backed by calm official confidence and useful visitor planning.**

The homepage must balance five visitor needs:

1. Inspire me.
2. Help me understand Sabah.
3. Show me what matches my interests.
4. Help me plan confidently.
5. Let me verify important information.

# 4. VISUAL AND CREATIVE DIRECTION

## 4.1 Overall art direction

Create a premium destination platform with:

- immersive editorial photography;
- strong visual hierarchy;
- generous breathing space;
- refined asymmetric composition where appropriate;
- natural transitions between cinematic and practical sections;
- restrained use of decorative motifs;
- excellent legibility over imagery;
- visible official trust without bureaucratic heaviness;
- a sense of discovery and movement; and
- a distinct Sabah identity.

Avoid:

- generic palm-tree tourism graphics;
- excessive gradients;
- oversaturated tropical colours;
- fake luxury styling;
- crowded card walls;
- glassmorphism everywhere;
- excessive rounded bubbles;
- heavy black boxes;
- tiny grey text;
- low-contrast text on photographs;
- icon overload;
- random wildlife collages;
- stereotyped or costume-like treatment of Sabah communities;
- stock photographs from places outside Sabah;
- invented landmarks, animals, traditions or environments;
- AI imagery with incorrect anatomy, geography, clothing or cultural details; and
- visual language that resembles Bali, Hawaii, Thailand, the Maldives or New Zealand instead of Sabah.

## 4.2 Provisional colour direction

Create a sophisticated exploratory palette inspired by Sabah, while treating it as a design proposal pending formal brand approval:

- **Rainforest Deep Green** — authority, forest and conservation;
- **South China Sea / Celebes Sea Blue** — depth, water and trust;
- **Tropical Turquoise** — discovery and marine experience;
- **Kinabalu Dawn Gold** — warmth, sunlight and primary emphasis;
- **Earth / Woven Fibre Brown** — heritage, craft and grounded warmth;
- **Cloud White** — space and clarity;
- **Charcoal** — primary text;
- **Mist Grey** — background separation.

Use colour selectively. The website must not become a rainbow because the Five Wonders have different identities. Maintain one coherent master brand.

Provide accessible contrast combinations. Do not place white text directly over a bright sky, white water or visually noisy image without a controlled overlay or text-safe composition.

## 4.3 Typography direction

Use an elegant, open-source, multilingual-compatible typography system:

- a confident editorial display face for major emotional headlines;
- a highly legible humanist sans-serif for navigation, body text, controls and practical information;
- proper support/fallback for Simplified Chinese;
- clear differentiation between headings, descriptions, metadata and controls;
- comfortable body sizes and line lengths;
- no ultra-thin typography over images.

Suggested direction only: a refined editorial serif paired with a modern humanist sans, supported by the Noto family for multilingual consistency. Do not sacrifice Chinese or Bahasa Malaysia readability for decorative English typography.

# 5. VISITSABAH.NET LOGO DESIGN

Design an original VisitSabah.net logo specifically for the website.

## 5.1 Logo concept

Create a refined symbol based on **five continuous natural gestures** representing:

1. Peak;
2. Deep;
3. Wild;
4. Ancient; and
5. Culture.

The five gestures may subtly form:

- a forward-moving “V”;
- a mountain-to-sea silhouette;
- a topographic or woven rhythm; or
- an abstract gateway opening into Sabah.

The symbol must remain simple enough to work at favicon size. It should communicate landscape, connection and discovery without trying to draw five literal pictures.

## 5.2 Wordmark

Use the exact name:

> **VisitSabah.net**

Treatment:

- “VisitSabah” should be the dominant wordmark;
- “.net” may be slightly smaller or use an accent treatment, but it must remain clearly readable;
- use confident upper/lowercase rather than an overly corporate all-capital treatment;
- the logo should feel welcoming, contemporary and official;
- it must be suitable for English, Chinese and Bahasa Malaysia versions without redesigning the mark.

Optional descriptor, used outside the core logo:

> **The Official Sabah Visitor Experience Gateway**

## 5.3 Logo restrictions

Do not:

- copy or redraw the Sabah state crest;
- imitate the Sabah Tourism Board logo;
- use a generic airplane, location pin, palm tree or globe;
- combine a mountain, hornbill, turtle, rafflesia, longhouse and wave into a complicated crest;
- use photorealistic illustrations;
- make the logo dependent on five different colours;
- use cultural patterns without proper provenance and approval; or
- imply an official government seal that has not been authorised.

Show the VisitSabah.net identity in the header, with any government/agency endorsement placed separately and discreetly.

Follow the sequence below. Do not randomly reorder sections.

## SECTION 1 — CINEMATIC DESTINATION HERO

### Purpose

Immediately establish Sabah’s emotional distinction, official identity and two most valuable visitor actions.

### Desktop layout

- near-full viewport hero, approximately 760–880 px tall depending on composition;
- use a Z-pattern:
  - identity/navigation begins upper-left;
  - emotional headline occupies the left or centre-left;
  - primary action resolves toward the lower-right/primary visual path;
- cinematic edge-to-edge Sabah photograph or controlled five-scene visual sequence;
- dark-to-transparent image overlay only where required for readability;
- visible carousel/sequence pagination and pause control;
- restrained scroll cue.

### Hero content

Eyebrow:

> **The Official Sabah Visitor Experience Gateway**

Primary H1:

> **Sabah. Peak. Deep. Wild. Ancient. Culture.**

Supporting copy:

> **Journey from Mount Kinabalu and coral seas to wildlife-rich rivers, ancient rainforests and Sabah’s living cultures. Discover what calls you, then plan with trusted visitor information.**

Primary CTA:

> **Explore Sabah**

Secondary CTA:

> **Build My Trip**

Assistance action:

> **Ask Sabah**

Optional small trust line:

> **Official destination inspiration, planning guidance and recognised tourism services.**

### Hero five-scene imagery

Use only authentic Sabah imagery:

1. **Peak** — Mount Kinabalu at first light, photographed with dramatic atmospheric depth and a composition that preserves a safe text area.
2. **Deep** — a diver above a healthy Sabah reef or blue-water drop-off, with strong shafts of light and genuine marine scale.
3. **Wild** — a respectful telephoto wildlife or Kinabatangan river scene showing habitat, not an unethical close encounter.
4. **Ancient** — mist lifting through Danum Valley or another verified Sabah rainforest canopy, emphasising age, scale and living ecology.
5. **Culture** — a genuine, consent-based moment of Sabah community life, craft, food, music or celebration, represented with dignity and specificity.

Do not show all five as five small equal boxes. Create one dominant cinematic story. If a carousel is used, each scene should carry a subtle Wonder label and the same persistent core CTA.

### Hero motion annotation

Recommend slow cinematic cross-dissolves, depth-aware image movement or elegant mask transitions. Avoid rapid autoplay, aggressive zooming or disorienting parallax. Include a pause control and respect reduced-motion settings.

### Mobile treatment

- use a carefully selected portrait crop rather than shrinking the desktop image;
- place text in a controlled lower gradient panel;
- keep H1 concise and dominant;
- stack CTAs full-width or use one full-width primary and one clear secondary;
- keep the Ask Sabah floating button outside the text block;
- avoid placing carousel controls under the bottom navigation;
- keep the important mountain, wildlife subject or human face out of the crop danger zone.

# 11. ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS

Design toward **WCAG 2.2 AA**.

Apply:

- sufficient text and component contrast;
- visible keyboard focus;
- logical heading order;
- only one H1;
- descriptive text links;
- icon plus text for important controls;
- labels that do not disappear after input;
- accessible carousel controls;
- pause for autoplay;
- reduced-motion support;
- alt-text fields for every image;
- captions/transcripts for video;
- non-map alternatives;
- status text in addition to colour;
- no text embedded permanently inside images;
- no essential information revealed only on hover;
- no flag-only language choice in the expanded state;
- scalable text without clipping;
- touch-target spacing;
- screen-reader labels in design annotations;
- accessible modal/drawer focus behaviour;
- clear error and recovery states.

Do not use disabled-looking low-contrast copy for ordinary body text.

- visually world-class;
- emotionally powerful without exaggeration;
- easy to understand within seconds;
- useful after the initial visual impact;
- credible as an official visitor platform;
- coherent with a website containing hundreds of CMS-driven pages;
- strong on both desktop and mobile;
- inclusive and accessible;
- ready for manual refinement by a professional designer;
- suitable for later implementation with a headless CMS.

Before finalising, audit the design against these questions:

1. Can a first-time visitor understand what Sabah offers within five seconds?
2. Are Peak, Deep, Wild, Ancient and Culture clearly introduced?
3. Are Explore Sabah and Build My Trip visually prioritised?
4. Is the official status visible without making the page bureaucratic?
5. Can a visitor begin by interest, place, map or itinerary?
6. Are SET, STEP and Visit Sabah 2027 visible but not allowed to overwhelm destination discovery?
7. Are practical information and trust available before the footer?
8. Is Ask Sabah persistent but non-obstructive?
9. Does every section have one clear purpose and next action?
10. Is all essential content preserved on mobile?
11. Are text and controls readable over every photograph?
12. Are cultural and wildlife images represented ethically?
13. Does the page avoid looking like a generic travel template?
14. Can every featured item be driven by CMS data?
15. Can the design tolerate English, Simplified Chinese and Bahasa Malaysia?

If any answer is no, refine the design before presenting it.
